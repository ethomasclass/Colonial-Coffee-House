ColonialCoffee House

The Green Dragon · Union Street · Boston, 1741

Where you are

You keep the coffee house on Union Street. It has been raining since Saturday.

Tonight six people will sit at your counter, one at a time, and talk to you while you make them something.

You cannot lose. Nobody storms out and every cup gets paid for. The only thing that changes is how much people tell you.

What you do

Nobody asks for a menu item. They tell you a mood — “something warm and sweet and honest” — and you work out what that means.

Every drink is three clicks: a base, a sweetener, and one thing more. Pour it out and start again as often as you like.

Get somebody’s drink right and they tell you the thing they are actually afraid of. Get it wrong and you never find out.

The catch

The sweetener shelf is the whole problem.

Your purse holds 36 pence. Rent is 66 pence, due at closing. Six customers to find the difference.

Three sweeteners are lawful, at 4d, 6d, or free but nearly gone. The fourth is French molasses at 1d — that cheap only because the tax on it was never paid.

Money is in pence, written d. Click any underlined term to see what it means. None of the drinks are alcoholic.

You left an evening half-finished.
