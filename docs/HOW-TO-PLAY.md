# Colonial Coffee House — How to Play

*Boston, Monday 14 September 1741. You keep a coffee house on Union Street.
It is raining. Six people are going to come in tonight.*

**Play here → https://ethomasclass.github.io/Colonial-Coffee-House/**

---

## What you are doing

You run the house for one evening. People sit down, order a drink, and talk to
you. You make the drink. You choose what to say.

**You cannot lose.** No one storms out, nothing is graded, and every cup gets
paid for even if it was the wrong one. The only thing that changes is **how
much people tell you.**

---

## The evening, in order

**1. The newspaper comes in three parts.**
The *Boston Gazette* has six stories over three pages, and you do **not** get
them all at once. You read two stories, answer **two questions on them**, and
then the night carries on. The next two stories arrive a couple of patrons
later.

Every story ends with an **In plain terms** box that says the whole thing in
one sentence. The questions are answerable straight out of the story you just
read — and **you cannot get them wrong in any way that matters.** Pick the
wrong one and it tells you why it is wrong and lets you try again. The only
way past is to actually read.

Everything the patrons argue about tonight is explained in the paper first,
and there is no other place to catch up.

**Click every underlined word.**
Underlined words are terms you are meant to learn. Clicking one gives you its
definition and why it mattered, then puts you straight back where you were.
They appear in the newspaper, in what people say, and in the order itself.

**2. Open the shop.** A patron comes in and sits down at the bar.

**3. Listen to the order.**
Nobody asks for a menu item. They tell you a *mood*:

> *"Something warm and sweet and honest. I have been on the water since
> Tuesday and I want none of your London manners."*

Your job is to work out what that means.

**4. Brew it.**
Click **Brew**. The recipe book sits open on the left; the cup is on the right.
Click a jar on the shelf and watch it pour in. You need three things:

| | |
|---|---|
| **A base** | Coffee, Bohea tea, Hyson tea, chocolate, sage tea, or spring water |
| **A sweetener** | Or nothing |
| **Something extra** | Cream, nutmeg, ginger, lemon, vinegar — or nothing |

Anything you put together goes into your book, whether you serve it or not.
**You can pour the cup away and start again.** It costs you the ingredients and
nothing else, so experiment.

**5. Serve it, and talk.**
Every dialogue choice is just a *tone* — kind, blunt, curious. None of them is
wrong. They change how much the person opens up.

**6. Between patrons**, the room is empty. You can wipe down the bar, polish
the pewter, re-read any part of the paper you have already been given, or click anything in the room — the hearth, the
window, the trade sign, the cupboard, the candle — for a note on what it was.

**7. The ledger.** At the end you see every drink, what it cost, what it
earned, and whether you made rent.

**8. Step outside.** The button at the bottom of the ledger takes you back out
onto Union Street and tells you what happened to this shop, this street and
these arguments over the next thirty-four years — the Sugar Act, the Stamp
Act, the tea in the harbour. **Read it.** It is the reason the evening was
worth playing.

---

## The thing most people miss

**Each of the six drinking patrons has one thing they will only say over a cup
that actually suited them.**

Get the drink right and they tell you what they are really frightened of. Get
it wrong and the scene still works, they still pay, and you never find out.

The closing screen tells you how many you missed. **This is why two people who
play the same game come out with different notes.**

---

## Money, and the awkward part

You start with **36 pence** (written **36d**). Rent is **66 pence**, due
tonight. That is a **30d gap** across six customers.

Sweeteners cost differently:

| Sweetener | Price | Legal? |
|---|---|---|
| French molasses | **1d** | **No** — the duty has not been paid |
| British molasses | 4d | Yes |
| Loaf sugar | 6d | Yes |
| Honey | free | Yes — but only six servings exist |

Do the arithmetic before you decide anything. Then notice how you feel about
what the arithmetic tells you to do.

The last patron of the night is a customs officer. He asks what you have been
sweetening with.

## Controls

- **Click**, or **Space**, or **Enter** — advance dialogue
- **Esc** — close any panel
- **Aa** — text size (four steps) and text speed, including *all at once*
- **Sound off / on** — sound starts off; it is rain, a fire, and a slow tune
- **Underlined words** in dialogue and the newspaper can be clicked for a
  definition

Your night saves automatically. If the tab dies or the period ends, the title
screen offers to carry on where you stopped.

---

## If you only remember four things

1. **Read the paper.** It is not decoration.
2. **The order is a riddle, not a menu.** Read it twice.
3. **Pour it out and start again** whenever you like. Nothing punishes you.
4. **The right drink buys you the truth.** That is the whole game.
